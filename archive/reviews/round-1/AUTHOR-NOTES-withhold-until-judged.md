# Author notes — withhold from the judge until they have scored

Sharing this file before the review will bias the result. It exists so you can
compare the independent verdict against the prior one.

## Prior recommendation (from the design session)

Ship **A as the reader**, **C as the print/PDF and archival edition**, and
**B as the daily-notification surface**. Not rivals — three surfaces over one
data model. Format: **PWA first**, with a plain EPUB exported from the same JSON
for people who want it.

## Where I'd expect a good judge to push back

- **B's split screen is desktop-shaped.** The strongest thing about B — deck
  driving text — is exactly what dies at the mobile breakpoint, which is the
  stated primary context. A reviewer who catches that is reading carefully.
- **A is the least distinctive.** It is the safest and probably the right default,
  but nothing in it is novel except Focus mode.
- **C may be too reverent to use daily.** Beautiful as an edition, possibly inert
  as a habit.
- **The essence points are editorial invention.** Eight summary bullets I wrote.
  They are not in the book. A judge weighing reverence heavily may object to
  presenting them at the same visual level as the author's text — and would have
  a point.
- **Design B's aura/glow and auto-advance** are the two features most at risk of
  reading as "app-like" against a sacred text.

## Things I know are imperfect and did not list as "known limitations"

Left off that list deliberately, so the judge can find them:

- A's floating pager overlaps body text at some viewport heights.
- B's ✦ Marked filter is ON by default, which means B opens showing 11 of 23
  sentences in the deck. Defensible, but it is a strong default.
- C's marginal notes can crowd when several underlines fall close together; the
  collision nudge is crude.
- Colour contrast has not been measured against WCAG in either theme.

## Questions worth asking the judge afterwards

1. Which single mechanic would you carry into whichever design wins?
2. Does the underline treatment ever imply the marks are the author's emphasis?
3. If you had to ship one artifact to a 70-year-old Marathi reader tomorrow,
   which, and what would you change first?
